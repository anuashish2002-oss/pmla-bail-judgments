Doctrinal Evolution Analysis (2021–2026)

Phase I · Jul 2022 – Mar 2024

Statutory Rigour & Revival of Twin Conditions

Anchored by the three-judge bench ruling in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India (2022 INSC 757), the Supreme Court upheld the 2018 legislative revival of the twin bail conditions under Section 45(1) PMLA and extended them to anticipatory bail under Section 438 CrPC. In M. Gopal Reddy (2023 INSC 163), Aditya Tripathi (2023 INSC 531), Tarun Kumar (2023 INSC 1006), Saumya Chaurasia (2023 INSC 1073), and Satyendar Kumar Jain (2024 INSC 217), the Court strictly enforced the reverse-burden threshold and treated Section 50 PMLA statements as prima facie material at the bail stage.

Phase II · Oct 2023 – May 2024

Procedural Guardrails on Arrest & Cognizance

Recognizing the severity of Section 45, the Court shifted scrutiny to pre-bail procedural compliance. In Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India (2023 INSC 866), the Court mandated furnishing written grounds of arrest under Section 19(1) PMLA—clarified as prospective in Ram Kishor Arora (2023 INSC 1082). Subsequently, in Tarsem Lal v. Directorate of Enforcement (2024 INSC 434), the Court insulated accused appearing on post-cognizance summons under Section 44(1)(b) from both Section 19 arrest and the Section 45 bail filter.

Phase III · Aug 2024 – Jan 2026

Constitutional Supremacy of Article 21 Speedy Trial

Beginning with Manish Sisodia v. Directorate of Enforcement (2024 INSC 595), Prem Prakash (2024 INSC 637), and V. Senthil Balaji (2024 INSC 739), the Court established that Section 45(1)(ii) cannot authorize indefinite pre-trial incarceration where custody exceeds 14–17 months and hundreds of witnesses remain unexamined. While Union of India v. Kanhaiya Prasad (2025 INSC 210) confirmed that cryptic bail orders without delay findings remain impermissible, Udhaw Singh (2025 INSC 247) and Arvind Dham (2026 INSC 12) cemented the rule that offence gravity cannot eclipse Article 21.
